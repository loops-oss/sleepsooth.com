How to relax before bed: a 20-minute wind-down

Falling asleep is easier when your body and mind are already slowing down. This routine takes about 20 minutes, needs no equipment, and works for rest even on nights you do not sleep well.

To relax before bed, give yourself 20 minutes of wind-down: dim the lights and put screens away, write tomorrow's to-do list, do a few minutes of slow breathing with a longer exhale, relax each muscle group in turn, and play a quiet, steady sound if it helps.

The 20-minute wind-down

  1. Minute 0 to 3: lights down, screens away. Switch to warm, low lamps and put your phone out of reach. This is the cue that the day is over.
  2. Minute 3 to 6: write tomorrow's to-do list. In a sleep lab study, people who spent five minutes writing a to-do list fell asleep faster than those who wrote about tasks they had already finished (Scullin et al., 2018).
  3. Minute 6 to 11: slow breathing. Breathe in through your nose for about 4 seconds and out for about 6. Slow breathing near six breaths a minute is linked with calmer heart rate and mood (Zaccaro et al., 2018).
  4. Minute 11 to 17: progressive muscle relaxation. Starting at your feet, tense each muscle group gently for 5 seconds, then let it go for 10. Work up to your face.
  5. Minute 17 to 20: sound and stillness. Lie down with a quiet, steady sound such as ocean waves, light rain or singing bowls, and let your breathing follow it.

Rest counts, even when sleep does not come

Lying still with a relaxed body and slow breathing is restful, even if you stay awake. Trying hard to sleep usually backfires. Aim to relax, and let sleep arrive when it does. If you are still wide awake after about 20 minutes, get up, read something calm in dim light, and return when you feel sleepy.

Relaxation sounds to try

  • Ocean waves: one wave every 7 to 12 seconds is a natural breathing guide.
  • Tibetan singing bowls: long, fading tones for meditation and the last minutes before sleep.
  • Light rain or a river: soft, steady and easy to ignore.

Frequently asked questions

How can I relax my mind before bed?

Write tomorrow's to-do list to get tasks out of your head, then do a few minutes of slow breathing with a longer exhale than inhale.

What is the 4-6 breathing technique?

Breathe in for about 4 seconds and out for about 6, which works out to around six breaths a minute. A longer exhale helps many people feel calmer.

Is resting in bed as good as sleeping?

No, but it still helps. Quiet, relaxed rest lowers arousal and makes falling asleep more likely. Avoid forcing sleep; focus on relaxing instead.

What sounds are best for relaxation?

Slow, steady sounds: ocean waves, light rain, a stream or singing bowls. Choose one you find pleasant and keep it quiet.
