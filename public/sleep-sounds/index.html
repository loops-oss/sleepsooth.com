White, pink and brown noise for sleep: which sound is best?

Background sound is one of the simplest sleep aids there is. Here is how the common types differ, what the research actually shows, and how to use them without overdoing the volume.

The best sleep sound is a steady one you find easy to ignore. White noise is bright and hissy, pink noise is softer and balanced, and brown noise is deep and rumbly, like heavy rain or wind. Research suggests sound mainly helps by masking disruptive noise, and the evidence that it improves sleep overall is still limited.

Nature soundsMixtures of the above with natural variationRain, ocean waves, a stream, forest

What the research says

A 2021 systematic review of 38 studies rated the evidence that continuous noise improves sleep as very low quality (Riedy et al., 2021, Sleep Medicine Reviews). That does not mean it never helps; it means the studies are small and mixed. Where sound clearly helps is in masking: a steady sound makes sudden noises like traffic, doors or snoring less likely to wake you.

The exciting pink noise headlines come from a different technique. In lab studies, short bursts of pink noise timed to the brain's slow waves during deep sleep strengthened those waves and improved next-day memory (Ngo et al., 2013, Neuron; Papalambros et al., 2017). That needs equipment that reads your brain waves in real time, so it is not the same as playing pink noise all night.

Nature sounds vs noise

Rain, surf and wind are natural versions of pink and brown noise, with gentle variation that many people find more pleasant than a flat hiss. Others prefer pure noise precisely because it has no changes to notice. Try one for a week before switching.

How to use sleep sounds safely

  • Keep it quiet: just loud enough to soften background noise.
  • Put the speaker away from your head, across the room rather than on the pillow.
  • Use a timer if sound wakes you later in the night.
  • For babies, keep machines on low and well away from the cot. Some infant sleep machines exceeded safe levels at full volume when placed close (Hugh et al., 2014, Pediatrics).

Frequently asked questions

Is pink noise better than white noise for sleep?

Many people find pink noise more comfortable because it is less hissy. Lab studies of timed pink noise bursts are promising, but there is no strong evidence that playing pink noise all night beats white noise.

What is brown noise good for?

Brown noise is deep and rumbly, like heavy rain or wind. People use it to mask low background noise and many find it calming. Evidence is mostly anecdotal.

Is it bad to sleep with white noise every night?

For most adults it is fine at a low volume. Keep it quiet and the speaker away from your head. If you want to sleep without it eventually, wean down the volume gradually.

What volume should sleep sounds be?

Low: just loud enough to soften background noise and quieter than normal conversation.
